Plagiarism and AI-writing detectors share a blind spot: they analyze the
_document_, not the _student_. Existing tools answer "Is this original?" — none
answer "Did you write this?"

Author Proof asks the second question. On submission, it generates a
personalized comprehension quiz from the student's own writing, in the
submission's own language. The result surfaces as a score badge inside
SpeedGrader, where instructors already work — with a link out to the full
session for anyone who wants to dig deeper.

![The student comprehension quiz flow](/case-studies/author-proof-student-flow.svg)

I led this end-to-end on a 4–6 week timeline: the Canvas-embedded quiz-settings
panel, the AI-generated questions, the student flow, and the SpeedGrader badge →
Review Center pathway. The design had to survive Canvas's iframe, FERPA, and one
awkward constraint — SpeedGrader's badge color is Canvas-controlled and runs
_inverted_ against our scoring, so an 80% shows red. That got resolved with
color-independent copy rather than a platform override we didn't control.

Throughout, the product had to read as **confidence, not verdict** — evidence
for a human conversation, never an automated judgment.
